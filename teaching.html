Teaching Expierience

I have had the great privilege of having the role of a teacher in a handful of classrooms throughout my career. Each has been an enriching experience for me (I hope my students would say the same). Learning physics, astronomy, or mathematics is a great arena to learn about oneself. An obvious benefit is exercising one's critical thinking ability to solve a problem in front of you, but for some of the classes that I have taught, the benefit in learning how to do physics problems is sometimes lost on my students. Some get inundated with the rigor, procedure, and the jargon, which I agree can become exhausting. We've heard the age-old phrase uttered by most students in an algebra class looking up from a trigonometry problem: "but when will I need this?" I love that question. If I have done right by my students, even if they won't largely go into STEM fields, they will carry the lessons learned in my class with them through their careers. Critical thinking and problem solving, logic, quantitative reasoning certainly. But also creativity, ingenuity, perseverance, and sometimes courage are some of the keystone lessons that one can learn in a STEM class.
Throughout my career, I have been a teaching assistant and instructor in electricity & magnetism laboratory classes for physics majors and in a general education setting. I have also frequently taught in introductory physics classes that had a team-based learning classroom where students are seated in groups in a classroom in an effort to depart from more didactic pedagogies. I have taught an intro astronomy course for the past few years titled "Observations of the Sky" where I try to have students reckon with everyday phenomena that they might take for granted, and hopefully understand the profound significance of the human understanding of simple things like the cycle of day and night. I have been a teaching assistant for many other courses, such as intro E&M for majors and computational physics.
