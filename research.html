High-Energy Astrophysics

Gamma-ray Observations

Understanding non-thermal physics through high-energy emission from astrophysical sources

overview

It was said to me once that anything done in high-energy astronomy is in service of understanding the generation and propagation of cosmic rays. Understanding the sources of ultra-high-energy cosmic rays (UHECRs) is vital to understanding energy transport in the universe. My work attempts to answer these questions by considering the observations from the Large Area Telescope aboard the Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope (Fermi-LAT). Generally, my work focuses on stellar remnants that are/were in binaries, namely millisecond pulsars in globular clusters and erupting white dwarf stars in cataclysmic variables (novae). In both of these cases particles are being accelerated to relatavistic speeds and become UHECRs. These CRs interact with the ambient universe, producing gamma rays observable by Fermi-LAT.
